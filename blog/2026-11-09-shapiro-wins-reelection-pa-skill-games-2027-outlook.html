Legislative Update

Shapiro Wins Reelection: What the November 3 Vote Means for Pennsylvania Skill Games in 2027

By Skill Games Pennsylvania • November 9, 2026 • 10 min read

Josh Shapiro won reelection as Pennsylvania governor on November 3, 2026, defeating Republican Stacy Garrity by approximately 22 percentage points — roughly 60 to 38 percent. For Pennsylvania skill game operators, the result carries a direct consequence: the administration’s long-standing 52 percent gross terminal revenue tax position does not change. The industry spent millions in the final weeks of the campaign backing Garrity, whose platform included a $500 monthly flat-fee-per-terminal framework. That bet did not pay off. Shapiro’s second term begins January 2027, the same month the new legislative session opens, and every operator planning for a path back to legal operation needs to understand exactly what that combination means.

The Election Results and What Was at Stake for Skill Games

Statewide results called on election night showed Shapiro with approximately 3.01 million votes to Garrity’s 1.91 million, a margin that CBS News, WITF, and other outlets called handily in Shapiro’s favor. Libertarian Kenneth V. Krawchuk drew roughly 1.3 percent of the vote.

Skill games were an explicit flashpoint in the closing weeks of the race. Garrity had hammered Shapiro repeatedly in October over the administration’s failure to resolve the skill games debate before the Supreme Court’s October 13 deadline expired and enforcement began on approximately 70,000 terminals. Her position — a $500 monthly flat fee per terminal, mirroring the structure of the bipartisan SB 1079 framework championed by Sens. Gene Yaw (R-23rd) and Anthony Williams (D-8th) — was substantially more operator-friendly than Shapiro’s 52 percent gross terminal revenue rate. Industry insiders responded by directing millions of dollars into Garrity’s campaign in the final weeks, an influx that reporting described as an effort to shift the race on the strength of this single issue.

It was not enough. Shapiro won by a wider margin than many pre-election polls suggested. The practical implication for the 2027 legislative session is that the administration’s opening position in any skill games tax negotiation remains 52 percent GTR — the rate that has been the single biggest obstacle to a legislative deal over two years of failed negotiations.

What Changed in the Legislature: The Senate Flip and House Composition

Beyond the governorship, the November 3 elections reshuffled the General Assembly in ways that matter for skill games. Multiple election tracking outlets, including MultiState, reported that Democrats won a state Senate majority for the first time in 48 years, though independent confirmation of the full Senate result was still being tabulated in the days after the election. Prior to November 3, Republicans held a 27–23 edge in the Senate.

Democrats held their narrow House majority, which had sat at 103 Democrats to 100 Republicans before the election. Across all 228 legislative races on the ballot statewide, approximately 15 seats changed party and 14 incumbents lost, according to MultiState’s early tabulation.

If the reported Senate Democratic majority holds through final certification, the 2027 legislative session will begin with Democrats controlling both chambers and the governor’s office — unified government for the first time in a generation. For skill games, the implications are not straightforward, and operators should resist reading a Democratic trifecta as either an automatic win or an automatic loss for their interests.

The clearest argument for a 2027 deal becoming easier: a unified Democratic government eliminates one source of partisan gridlock. The principal tax-rate deadlock in 2026 was not a straight partisan split — SB 1079’s bipartisan flat-fee structure had both Republican (Yaw) and Democratic (Williams) sponsors — but unified government does reduce the procedural obstacles to moving legislation when leadership decides to prioritize it. A Democratic Senate majority aligned with a Democratic governor is more likely to find a framework both chambers can pass than the split government that repeatedly failed in 2024, 2025, and 2026.

The counterargument: a Democratic majority in the Senate may be more sympathetic to Shapiro’s 52 percent GTR position than a Republican majority was, reducing pressure on the administration to compromise downward. The question for the 2027 session is whether a unified Democratic government converges on Shapiro’s rate or whether the wing of the Democratic caucus that supported bipartisan flat-fee frameworks — Sen. Williams was a SB 1079 co-sponsor — has enough influence to pull negotiations toward a lower number.

What Happens to the Bills That Failed in 2026

Pennsylvania’s 2025–2026 legislative session ends November 30, 2026. Under the state’s legislative rules, all bills pending at the close of a session expire and must be refiled in the new session. That means every skill games bill that failed to advance in 2026 — SB 1079, HB 2213, HB 2801, HB 2802, and Rep. Brad Roae’s emergency extension bill — is legally dead after November 30. Their sponsors must introduce new legislation in the 2027 session for any of those frameworks to have any force.

Historically, this procedural reset does not mean starting from zero. The policy work, stakeholder negotiations, and bill language developed across two years of failed legislation form the foundation for whatever refiled versions emerge in January 2027. Operators who followed the 2026 debate closely will recognize the frameworks that reappear. What changes is the political context in which they compete.

The two frameworks most likely to be refiled early in the 2027 session are:

The flat-fee bipartisan framework (successor to SB 1079 / HB 2213): A $500-per-terminal monthly fee, payable to the Pennsylvania Gaming Control Board, with proceeds split between the state and host municipalities. This structure has genuine bipartisan support — Yaw and Williams have both indicated ongoing interest — and is the closest thing to a consensus framework the industry has seen. It is significantly more operator-friendly than a GTR-based tax because the fee is fixed regardless of machine performance, making it possible for lower-volume locations to remain economically viable.

The Diamond GTR framework (successor to HB 2801 / HB 2802): A 20 percent gross terminal revenue tax restricted to liquor-licensed venues with a cap of five machines per location. Rep. Russ Diamond (R-Lebanon) introduced this proposal in the final weeks of the 2026 session. It is higher than the flat fee on a per-machine basis at typical revenue levels but substantially lower than Shapiro’s 52 percent and closer to the 16 percent rate some industry advocates had supported in earlier sessions.

Shapiro’s administration has not publicly moved from its 52 percent position, but the enforcement reality — approximately 70,000 machines removed from the market since October 14 and an estimated hundreds of millions in annual revenue no longer flowing through the state’s economy — creates at least a fiscal argument for a lower compromise rate. Whether the administration acts on that argument in 2027 is the central open question for operators.

The November 30 Session Close: Nothing to Expect Before January

With three weeks remaining in the 2026 session after the election, the realistic probability of lame-duck skill games legislation is very low. The same underlying disagreements over the tax rate that blocked action before October 13 remain unresolved, and there is no new pressure point that would drive a legislative breakthrough in three weeks that did not arrive in the 120 days before it. The more productive use of those three weeks, from the industry’s perspective, is building the relationships and prefiling preparation that positions the strongest possible bills for introduction on day one of January 2027.

Operators should not wait for a November surprise. The strategic focus belongs on January.

What This Means for Operators Waiting to Redeploy

For operators who removed machines in advance of October 13 and are now planning their 2027 return, the election results do not change the fundamental outline of what comes next — but they clarify the political landscape that will shape it.

The optimistic scenario for operators is a first-half 2027 deal at a rate between 20 and 40 percent GTR, or a flat-fee structure close to SB 1079’s model, signed by Shapiro and followed by an implementation window of 60 to 90 days before machines can legally return to locations. The pessimistic scenario is a repeat of 2025 and 2026: competing frameworks, caucus deadlocks, and a session that ends in June or later without a deal.

The most important thing operators can do between now and January is maintain the network of location relationships built during operating years. A bar that hosted machines for three years, a truck stop that relied on the revenue-share income, a VFW that used the proceeds to fund operations — these are the constituency that creates legislative pressure for a deal. Operators who stay in contact with their location partners, communicate a realistic timeline for 2027, and document their placement agreements are the ones who redeploy fastest when a framework is signed.

Specific actions for the weeks ahead:

Contact your legislators now. The election created a new General Assembly. Many of the members who will vote on skill games legislation in January 2027 are either newly elected or returning to changed conditions. Constituent pressure from business owners and location operators is most effective when it is established early in a session, not concentrated around a deadline. Contact your state representative and senator before the new session opens.

Track bill prefiling. Pennsylvania legislators may begin prefiling bills for the 2027 session as early as November and December 2026. The first skill games bills of the new session may be introduced before January’s opening day. Follow the Pennsylvania General Assembly’s website for prefiled legislation. Bill numbers will be different from the 2026 versions, but the policy frameworks will be recognizable.

Assess your documentation. Any 2027 regulatory framework is likely to include a licensing or registration component with an application window before machines can legally return to locations. The operators who respond fastest to that window are the ones whose documentation — business entity records, prior placement agreements, location addresses, machine serial numbers and manufacturer certifications — is current and in one place before the application opens. Use the current downtime to organize those records.

Stay realistic about the timeline. The earliest realistic scenario for machines returning to locations is mid-2027 at the best case — a bill signed in the first quarter followed by a 60 to 90 day implementation window. A more conservative estimate is late 2027 if the session follows a similar pattern to prior years. Plan your location relationships and business structure around a mid-to-late 2027 redeployment window rather than assuming an early 2027 breakthrough.

The Longer View: Why a Deal Still Gets Done

Shapiro’s reelection and the administration’s unchanged 52 percent position are real obstacles to a quick, operator-friendly resolution. But the structural forces that drove this industry to 70,000 machines and hundreds of millions in annual revenue have not disappeared. The demand that filled those locations still exists. The bars, convenience stores, VFWs, and fire companies that hosted machines and shared in the revenue still want them back. The state treasury is now collecting zero from a market that could generate substantial annual tax revenue at any rate above zero.

Pennsylvania has also watched every neighboring state navigate its own version of this fight. Virginia’s Governor Spanberger vetoed SB 661 in April 2026 for some of the same reasons Pennsylvania struggled — a fragmented regulatory picture and an inadequate consumer protection structure. Texas continues to operate in legal gray area. No state has found a perfect framework, but several have shown that a deal between the industry and state government is achievable when both sides accept a tax structure neither fully prefers.

The 2027 session will produce a skill games bill or it will not. What operators can control is how prepared they are to respond when it does, and how effectively they communicate their economic case to the legislators who will cast those votes. That work starts now.
